# Out-of-sample evidence on ML models for intraday crypto return prediction and trading (5-60 min horizons)

Method note: I could not open any full text directly in this session, because the fetch tool and curl were blocked from arxiv.org, gresearch.com and the other paper hosts. Every finding below comes from search-engine snippets and summaries of abstracts, conclusions and secondary write-ups. Exact table values (per-horizon accuracy, cost assumptions) are often unverified, and they are flagged as such. The report writer should treat the numbers as "as reported in abstract or snippet".

## Q1. What do peer-reviewed and working papers (2018-2026) report for intraday crypto ML prediction? Typical accuracies and ICs?

### Takeaway
Reported intraday directional accuracy usually sits a little above 50%. Careful studies with large samples report roughly 50-56%, and smaller or less careful studies report 55-66%. Claims of 70-80% or more come from very short (seconds-level) order-book horizons or from studies with weak validation. Correlation/IC-type scores on 15-minute residualised returns are tiny, about 0.02-0.05 on genuinely live data (G-Research).

### Cited Findings
- **Jaquart, Dann & Weinhardt (2021), *Journal of Finance and Data Science* 7:45-66.** BTC, horizons 1-60 min, several feature sets (technical, blockchain, sentiment, asset-based). All tested models beat a random classifier. Recurrent networks (LSTM/GRU) and gradient boosting performed best. Accuracy generally rose with horizon and was "above the 50% threshold" for all models. Technical features were the most relevant. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/350199855_Short-Term_Bitcoin_Market_Prediction_via_Machine_Learning); [Springer chapter (earlier version)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-64466-6_2). Exact per-horizon accuracies were not retrieved. From memory and unverified, they are in the low-to-mid 50s %.
- **Akyildirim, Goncu & Sensoy (2021), *Annals of Operations Research* 297:3-36.** 12 most liquid cryptos at daily and minute-level frequencies, using SVM, logistic regression, ANN and RF on past prices and technical indicators. Average accuracy was consistently above 50%, about 55-65% on average, and SVM performed best. — [ITU research portal](https://research.itu.edu.tr/en/publications/prediction-of-cryptocurrency-returns-using-machine-learning/); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Prediction-of-cryptocurrency-returns-using-machine-Akyildirim-G%C3%B6nc%C3%BC/d44efe577671bde0fd09105d9b1e2fdca047da94)
- **Akyildirim et al. (2021), "Forecasting mid-price movement of Bitcoin futures using machine learning", *Annals of Operations Research*.** Best out-of-sample success rate was 66%, reached by the monthly futures contract at the 15-min frequency. CME futures are a short, early sample. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8296834/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-021-04205-x)
- **Chen, Li & Sun (2020), "Bitcoin price prediction using machine learning: an approach to sample dimension engineering", *J. Computational and Applied Mathematics*.** XGBoost and similar models predict the 5-min price direction with 59.4% accuracy. Simple statistical methods do better on daily data. Uses an 80/20 single split, not walk-forward. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037704271930398X)
- **Alonso-Monsalve et al. (2020), *Expert Systems with Applications* 149:113250.** CNN, hybrid CNN-LSTM and MLP on 1-minute trend classification for 6 cryptos (BTC, DASH, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP), using 18 technical indicators and only one year of data. CNNs were judged beneficial for short-interval trend prediction. Exact accuracies not retrieved. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338940807_Convolution_on_Neural_Networks_for_High-Frequency_Trend_Prediction_of_Cryptocurrency_Exchange_Rates_Using_Technical_Indicators)
- **Jha (2020, arXiv 2010.01241), "Deep Learning for Digital Asset Limit Order Books".** Temporal CNNs on Coinbase BTC order-book data reach 71% walk-forward accuracy, but only at a **2-second** horizon. No cost or trading simulation appears in the snippets. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01241)
- **Gradojevic, Kukolj, Adcock & Djakovic (2023), "Forecasting Bitcoin with technical analysis: A not-so-random forest?", *International Journal of Forecasting* 39(1):1-17.** Random forest on technical indicators with expanding-window out-of-sample evaluation. A secondary summary says no model significantly beat a random walk at the hourly frequency, while RF did at daily. This is a third-party reading and unverified. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207021001230)
- **Multi-timeframe LightGBM paper (MDPI *Forecasting* 8(3):40, 2026).** All five models kept ROC-AUC above 0.54 on 1,136 out-of-sample samples from 2025. The best ROC-AUC was about 0.60. A multi-timeframe alignment (look-ahead) bug had inflated ROC-AUC by about 0.20. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-9394/8/3/40)
- **Comparable non-crypto benchmark: Mesfin (2026, arXiv 2605.17724).** 5-min MNQ futures bars, walk-forward over 2023-2025. Gradient boosting accuracy was 50.00-50.89% and LSTM 50.59%, against a 51.8% base rate. Permutation p-values were 0.135 (GBM) and 0.515 (LSTM), so neither is significant. This is a useful calibration for what honest 5-min OHLCV-only accuracy looks like. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17724)
- **G-Research Crypto Forecasting (Kaggle, Nov 2021 to May 2022).** Target: 15-minute-ahead residualised log returns for 14 coins. Metric: Pearson correlation per coin, weighted by log market cap. Ranked on about 3 months of live data after submissions closed. The final private-leaderboard snippet shows rank 6 at **0.0203**. The top private scores were not retrieved, but the gold-medal zone was clearly around 0.02-0.05. — [Kaggle leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/g-research-crypto-forecasting/leaderboard); [G-Research wrap-up](https://www.gresearch.com/news/wrapping-up-the-g-research-crypto-forecasting-competition/)
- **High-accuracy outliers.** Omole & Enke (2024, *Financial Innovation*) report Boruta + CNN-LSTM accuracy of 82.44% (precision 0.83, recall 0.81) for BTC direction, plus backtested annual returns of 437% (long-only) and 1084% (short-only). These are implausible as real tradeable results and are treated as upper bounds or likely leakage. Horizon and cost treatment were not confirmed. — [Springer / Financial Innovation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40854-024-00643-1)

### Inferences
- When samples are large, horizons are 5-60 min and validation is walk-forward, the credible range for directional accuracy is about 51-56%, which corresponds to ROC-AUC of about 0.52-0.60. Live IC on residualised 15-minute returns is about 0.02-0.05. Higher figures should prompt a check for leakage, class imbalance, smoothed labels, very short horizons or a single lucky test window.
- Accuracy rising with horizon (Jaquart) fits a picture where costs dominate at short horizons. A longer horizon gives a larger absolute move per trade, which can absorb fees.

### Gaps
- Exact per-horizon accuracy tables for Jaquart et al. and Alonso-Monsalve et al. (no full-text access).
- Top-3 private-LB scores for G-Research: the snippet started at rank 6.
- Few papers report IC or rank-IC for crypto intraday horizons. The main sources are the Kaggle metric and practitioner work.

## Q2. Which studies report profitability AFTER transaction costs, and how large is the edge relative to fees? Which collapse?

### Takeaway
Most intraday ML studies that model costs honestly find that net profits disappear. The gross edge per trade at 1-60 min horizons is usually a few basis points, while taker fees plus spread are about 10-30 bps per round trip. Positive net results survive only when trading is filtered hard to cut turnover (trade only when the predicted move is larger than the cost) or when the horizon is lengthened.

### Cited Findings
- **Jaquart et al. (2021, JFDS).** A quantile-based long-short strategy on BTC at 1-60 min horizons earns gross monthly returns of up to about 31% (the thesis version says up to 39%). Returns are **negative after transaction costs** because holding periods are so short. The authors conclude the results do not violate the EMH. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/350199855_Short-Term_Bitcoin_Market_Prediction_via_Machine_Learning); [d-nb.info thesis](https://d-nb.info/1276240287/34). The 31% vs 39% discrepancy between versions is noted.
- **Another short-term BTC study** (search snippet, likely the Jaquart line of work) found GBC had the highest accuracy at the 5-min horizon. Its long-short 1%-quantile returns were reported **before** costs over about 3 months of test data. — [KIT publication record](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000150665/149388567)
- **Bysik & Ślepaczuk (2026, arXiv 2606.00060 / SSRN 6795938).** About 70,000 hourly BTC-USDT observations (2018-2026). XGBoost, LSTM and iTransformer tested in a 27-fold walk-forward. Naive sign-based strategies show positive gross performance in some configurations but **collapse at 10 bps costs because they trade too frequently**. A cost-aware filter, which trades only when the forecast clears a cost threshold, cuts turnover and restores positive net performance in some XGBoost configurations. The best long-only XGBoost strategy reportedly made **annualised returns above 65% with Sharpe above 1**. Bootstrap tests do not show XGBoost formally dominating the other models, and loss-function and model-selection effects are "statistically fragile". The cost model is proportional only, with no order-book depth, partial fills, maker/taker routing or time-varying spread. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00060); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6795938). A DEV Community secondary post mentions a 10 bps round-trip baseline, a 0-25 bps cost grid and threshold λ=2.0. These are unverified. — [DEV post](https://dev.to/shaktitiwari/bitcoin-trading-with-xgboost-what-70000-hours-of-data-actually-prove-2026-walk-forward-evidence-ihh)
- **Multi-timeframe LightGBM BTC paper (MDPI Forecasting, 2026).** The event-driven backtest on 2025 out-of-sample data gives gross **+35.97%** over 185 trades (1% SL / 2% TP, 0.7 probability threshold), with Sharpe 0.14. At 0.2% round-trip fees, about 37 percentage points of drag would erase the profit. The net figure is the authors' estimate, not a separate backtest. The authors conclude that ROC-AUC of about 0.60 is not enough on its own. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-9394/8/3/40)
- **"Predictive Extrema, Unprofitable Policies" (July 2026, arXiv 2607.19453).** An audit of candle-based Binance Spot timing models (local-minimum and maximum entry/exit, daily selectors). They show strong ROC-AUC but weak average precision, and **negative net returns**. The strongest later-period test, a 19-cycle July campaign, lost 6.72% at an assumed 31 bps cost. The verdict is NO_TRADE for all policies. This is a preprint and was only seen via the Pith summary. — [Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2607.19453)
- **CRIX intraday return-curve paper (Finance Research Letters / IRFA, about 2021).** Functional-data prediction of intraday return curves with a rolling window, out-of-sample dates and an assumed transaction fee of 0.03%. That fee is very low compared with retail taker fees. — [Reading Centaur PDF](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/97607/1/Bitcoin_Functional_Accepted.pdf); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521921001228)
- **Turn-of-the-candle effect (2023, PMC).** 5-min BTC data show positive returns of about 0.58 bps per minute concentrated at 15-minute candle turns, and the effect persists out-of-sample. A sub-bp-per-minute edge like this is far below typical round-trip costs unless execution is maker-only. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10015199/)
- **Order-book deep learning (DeepLOB family).** The DeepLOB authors note that labels built from smoothed price changes produce inconsistent signals, causing redundant trades and higher costs. A 2024 LOB study found that when trends were redefined using the average spread as a cost proxy, performance deteriorated. — [DeepLOB](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327011035_DeepLOB_Deep_Convolutional_Neural_Networks_for_Limit_Order_Books); [arXiv 2409.14157](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.14157); [Quant. Finance 2025 microstructural guide](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2025.2522911)
- **Daily-horizon comparison (not intraday).** Sebastião & Godinho (2021, *Financial Innovation*) report ensemble after-cost annualised Sharpe of 80.17% (ETH) and 91.35% (LTC) at 0.5% round-trip costs. That is daily frequency and shows that net profitability is easier at lower turnover. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40854-020-00217-x)
- A replication-style LSTM BTC project found that, after costs, LSTM strategies do not generate significant risk-adjusted returns. — [GitHub upathare1](https://github.com/upathare1/Bitcoin-Price-Prediction)

### Inferences
- Rough break-even arithmetic: at 10 bps per round trip on BTC (taker plus spread), a 5-min strategy that trades every bar needs more than 10 bps of expected move per trade. Typical 5-min BTC absolute moves are on the order of 10-20 bps, so a 52-54% hit rate leaves a gross edge of only about 1-2 bps per trade. This is why naive strategies collapse. My arithmetic, not from a source.
- Positive net results come from (a) heavy signal-threshold filtering that cuts turnover, (b) longer horizons (hourly) and (c) long-only exposure, which can partly be a beta to BTC's 2018-2026 bull run. Bysik & Ślepaczuk's 65% annual return should be benchmarked against buy-and-hold over the same window.
- Maker-only execution or fee rebates are essential for anything at 5-15 min. No academic study found here models queue position or fill probability realistically.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that reports a net-of-cost Sharpe for a 5-15 min crypto ML strategy with realistic order-book execution.
- Drawdown and turnover numbers for Bysik & Ślepaczuk (full tables not retrieved).

## Q3. Do deep models (LSTM, Transformers) beat gradient boosting / linear models on tabular price features out-of-sample? Kaggle evidence?

### Takeaway
No clearly. On tabular OHLCV-type features, gradient boosting (LightGBM/XGBoost) matches or beats LSTM and Transformers out-of-sample, and the strongest competitive evidence (G-Research) was won by LightGBM. Feature engineering mattered more than model choice. Deep models show an advantage mainly on raw order-book data at second-level horizons.

### Cited Findings
- **G-Research Crypto Forecasting (Kaggle, 2021-22, $125k prize pool).** The **top 3 all used LightGBM**. Some entrants tried neural networks. G-Research said feature development "had a far greater impact on their final scores than model development". Winners: Meme Lord Capital (Jose and Eduardo, $50k), Nathaniel Maddux (2nd), team GABA (3rd). — [G-Research wrap-up](https://www.gresearch.com/news/wrapping-up-the-g-research-crypto-forecasting-competition/)
- **2nd-place solution.** A single LightGBM GBDT regressor with squared loss, no ensembling, and hyperparameters not exhaustively tuned. Public-LB scores were unrealistically high because of probing and overfitting (part of the test period overlapped with public data), so only private and live scores are meaningful. — [Kaggle discussion 323098](https://www.kaggle.com/c/g-research-crypto-forecasting/discussion/323098)
- A GitHub reimplementation reports a cross-validation mean of 0.2176 for LightGBM with own plus cross-asset features, against about 0.02 live. That is an order-of-magnitude gap between CV and live, illustrating overfitting/leakage risk. It is not a Kaggle private score. — [GitHub Wy66wy](https://github.com/Wy66wy/g-research-crypto-forecasting)
- **Jaquart et al. (2021).** On BTC at 1-60 min, recurrent nets and gradient boosting were the best classes. Neither dominated strongly. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/350199855_Short-Term_Bitcoin_Market_Prediction_via_Machine_Learning)
- **Bysik & Ślepaczuk (2026).** XGBoost was descriptively stronger than LSTM and iTransformer on hourly BTC, but bootstrap tests do not support formal dominance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00060)
- **Mesfin (2026), MNQ 5-min, non-crypto.** Neither GBM nor LSTM was significant. LSTM was no better than GBM. Conclusion: 5-min OHLCV sequences lack sufficient sequential structure at that data size. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17724)
- **Daily-horizon comparison.** Alessandretti et al. found gradient-boosted trees worked best on daily crypto with short windows. A 2025 comparison found gradient boosting consistently beat XGBoost, SVR and LSTM (on regression metrics). — [Springer Discover AI 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-025-00519-y)
- **Order-book deep learning.** Jha (2020) reached 71% accuracy at a 2-second horizon on Coinbase BTC LOB data with temporal CNNs. A Quantitative Finance (2025) microstructural guide on DeepLOB-type models finds that forecastability depends heavily on microstructure (tick size, liquidity) and is not easily turned into trading profit. — [arXiv 2010.01241](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01241); [Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2025.2522911)
- **Reinforcement learning.** Gort et al. (arXiv 2209.05559) show that many DRL crypto papers rely on walk-forward backtests and report optimistic profits. They propose estimating the probability of backtest overfitting (combinatorial CV) and rejecting overfitted agents. On 10 cryptos over May-June 2022, the less-overfitted PPO agent beat the overfitted agents, an equal-weight portfolio and a market benchmark. The test window is very short (2 months, during a bear market). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05559). Wang & Klabjan (arXiv 2309.00626) propose an ensemble DRL method for intraday crypto portfolios with multiple validation periods and periodic retraining. Cost details were not retrieved. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.00626)

### Inferences
- For a 5-60 min crypto bot built on candle and derived features, LightGBM/XGBoost is the evidence-backed default. Deep sequence models add cost and overfitting risk without demonstrated out-of-sample gain on tabular inputs.
- RL evidence is the weakest. Test windows are short, many papers do not handle costs carefully, and the overfitting probability is high.
- Ensembles: the G-Research 2nd place won with no ensemble, and I found no strong intraday evidence that ensembling beats a well-featured single GBM.

### Gaps
- 1st-place G-Research write-up: feature set and private score not retrieved.
- No rigorous crypto-specific minute-bar Transformer/TFT vs GBM study with walk-forward and costs was found, beyond Bysik & Ślepaczuk (hourly, iTransformer).
- Ensemble-specific intraday evidence is thin.

## Q4. How does predictability change over time (decay as markets mature)?

### Takeaway
The evidence points to declining predictability as crypto has matured, especially at daily horizons and after 2016. Intraday predictability (up to about 6 hours) persists but varies over time, with regime shifts such as 2019-20 vs 2021-22. Higher frequency means lower efficiency, but also higher relative costs.

### Cited Findings
- Urquhart (2016) found BTC returns highly predictable early on, with predictability shrinking as the market matured. Bariviera (2017) and Sensoy (2019) report similar results. — cited in [MDPI JRFM 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/19/9/678)
- **Sensoy (2019), Finance Research Letters.** Permutation-entropy tests on BTCUSD and BTCEUR show both markets have become more informationally efficient at the intraday level since early 2016, and that higher frequency means lower pricing efficiency. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612318302320); [Bilkent PDF](https://yoksis.bilkent.edu.tr/pdf/files/14366.pdf)
- **"Bitcoin at High Frequency" (JRFM 2019).** Using tick data from Bitstamp and Coinbase, there is no predictability at horizons of one day or more, but predictability exists at sampling frequencies up to 6 hours, and it is time-varying. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/12/1/36)
- **Stylized facts of HF Bitcoin (arXiv 2402.11930, 2024).** For 2019-2022 there are two distinct regimes (2019-20 and 2021-22) with an abrupt change in volatility, and a slightly higher autocorrelation decay rate in the second. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11930v2)
- **Intraday momentum/reversal (2022, North American J. Econ & Finance).** Intraday return predictability in crypto shows both momentum and reversal patterns. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1062940822000833)
- **G-Research live evaluation (2022).** Live private-LB correlations of about 0.02 compare with much higher public and CV scores. This is consistent with weak, fast-decaying signals out-of-sample. — [Kaggle leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/g-research-crypto-forecasting/leaderboard)
- **Regime dependence in ML.** Sebastião & Godinho (2021) explicitly study ML forecasting "under changing market conditions" and find performance varies across regimes. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40854-020-00217-x)

### Inferences
- Papers using 2016-2018 data (much of the early ML literature, e.g. Chen et al. 2020 and Alonso-Monsalve 2020) probably overstate what is achievable today. Models need frequent retraining (rolling or expanding walk-forward) and monitoring for alpha decay.

### Gaps
- No study found that directly measures ML intraday accuracy or IC decay year by year on a fixed methodology for 2018-2026.

## Cross-cutting methodological caveats (sample size, survivorship, leakage)
- Many studies use short samples (about 1 year, as in Alonso-Monsalve) or a single 80/20 split (Chen et al.), with no walk-forward. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037704271930398X); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338940807_Convolution_on_Neural_Networks_for_High-Frequency_Trend_Prediction_of_Cryptocurrency_Exchange_Rates_Using_Technical_Indicators)
- Look-ahead bias from multi-timeframe alignment inflated ROC-AUC by about 0.20 in one study. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-9394/8/3/40)
- Reviews call for walk-forward validation, leakage control, ablations, uncertainty reporting and cost-adjusted trading metrics, and note that the most accurate model is not necessarily the best trading model. — [Zenodo review](https://zenodo.org/records/20610140); [ScienceDirect review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772662225001262)
- Backtest overfitting in DRL: walk-forward on its own gives optimistic results, and combinatorial CV with PBO estimation is recommended. — [arXiv 2209.05559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05559)
- Survivorship: multi-coin studies usually pick today's "most liquid" coins (e.g. the top 12 in Akyildirim, 14 coins in G-Research), which builds survivorship into cross-sectional results. Inference; no source quantified it.
- Accuracy just above 50% can reflect class imbalance or trend (base-rate) rather than skill. Mesfin's 51.8% base rate shows that "51%" can be below the base rate. — [arXiv 2605.17724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17724)
